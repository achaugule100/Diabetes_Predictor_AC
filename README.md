Data Analytics for Accessible Diabetes Risk Prediction 
Jun 2025 - Aug 2025

Over the past year, I independently researched accessible diabetes risk prediction using machine learning. Using CDC-derived 2015 BRFSS metrics, I developed a Random Forest model inspired by my immediate family’s history of Type-2 Diabetes and its high prevalence in my community. Often, it can be hard to gauge diabetes risk, as diagnoses rely on inaccessible, laboratory-based indicators. To create the model, I applied feature engineering, label encoding, MinMax scaling, and class balancing techniques, achieving 75.3% accuracy and 80.3% recall. I also analyzed feature importance and correlations to identify key predictors in the data, including general health, BMI, blood pressure, and age, demonstrating how predictive analytics can support equitable, non-laboratory-based diabetes risk assessment. As a self-initiated project, it represents both my technical growth and my commitment to applying data science to challenges that matter to me personally and socially.
